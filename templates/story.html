The story of coffee in Colombia

How a crop planted on a few mountain farms became the heart of a country's culture, and one of the most loved coffees in the world.

Taste it yourself

A short timeline

Early 1700s

Coffee reaches Colombia. Tradition credits Jesuit missionaries with planting the first bushes in the eastern mountains.

1835

The first small commercial shipment leaves from Cúcuta, near the Venezuelan border. Colombia is now an exporter.

1927

Growers found the National Federation of Coffee Growers (FNC) to represent small farmers and protect quality.

1958

Juan Valdez, the farmer with his mule Conchita, becomes the face of 100% Colombian coffee.

2011

UNESCO names the Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia a World Heritage Site.

Why it tastes different

Colombia grows mostly Arabica on mountain slopes with rich soil, steady rain and cool nights. Most farms are small family plots, and ripe cherries are picked by hand. The result is a cup that is smooth, sweet and balanced.
